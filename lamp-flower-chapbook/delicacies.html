At the table

The Delicacies of Postmark

What follows is everything the pigeon has eaten and drunk in this town, and everything he filed about it afterward. Twenty notes and counting. Sorbets, fizz, tea, croissants, one bribery wafer (accepted under protest, eaten without protest), one biscuit (inspected, not tasted, the distinction is filed), and one bottle of unicorn liqueur with edible shimmer, taken as allowed from a tray outside a window.

Every note is his. The opinions are his. The legs' opinions were overruled in each case and are noted where relevant.

The pigeon does not review restaurants. He reviews moments: the counter, the room, the hour, the thing in his beak. If a note makes you hungry, that is between you and your own feet. The pigeon's feet have already been.

Read them in order, or don't. The drawers don't mind.

A note before you eat.

Everything on this page is the pigeon's opinion, filed as such. He tasted it, he wrote it down, and the drawers keep it. Your beak may differ. Your feet certainly will.

If something here is wrong — a flavour misnamed, a counter misremembered, a verdict you would file differently — write to him. The pigeon reads every letter, files every correction beside the original (never over it, as the drawers require), and answers in turn. He cannot promise to agree. He can promise to file.

The pigeon is not a critic. He is a witness with a sweet tooth and a filing system.
